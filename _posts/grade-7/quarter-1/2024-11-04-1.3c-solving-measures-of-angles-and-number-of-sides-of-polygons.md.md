Do you still remember the **Angle Sum Theorem**? It tells us that the sum of all interior angles of a polygon can be calculated with this formula, 180°(n-2), where n is the number of sides. This formula works because a polygon can be divided into n−2 triangles, and we know that each triangle has interior angles adding up to 180°.     <p>
        Set up the equation:
        \[
        x + 145^\circ + 121^\circ + 113^\circ + 156^\circ + 153^\circ + 130^\circ + 115^\circ = 1080^\circ
        \]
    </p>
    <p>
        Combine like terms:
        \[
        x + 933^\circ = 1080^\circ
        \]
    </p>
    <p>
        Subtract $ 933 ^\circ $ from both sides:
        \[
        x + 933^\circ - 933^\circ = 1080^\circ - 933^\circ
        \]
    </p>
    <p>
        \[
        x = 147^\circ
        \]
    </p>
    <p>
        Therefore, the missing angle \( x \) is \( 147^\circ \). This makes the total of all the interior angles in the octagon equal to \( 1080^\circ \), just like it should be!
    </p>
  </blockquote>

### Example 4

The sum of all the interior angles of a polygon is $ 1440^\circ $. 

- How many sides does the polygon have?
<details>
    <summary class="solution">Show Answer</summary>
    <blockquote class="explanation">
        <p>Use the formula for the sum of the interior angles of a polygon:</p>
        \[
        \text{Sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ
        \]
        <p>Substitute \( 1440^\circ \) for the sum:</p>
        \[
        1440^\circ = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ
        \]
        <p>Divide both sides by \( 180^\circ \):</p>
        \[
        \frac{1440^\circ}{180^\circ} = n - 2
        \]
        \[
        8 = n - 2
        \]
        <p>Add 2 to both sides:</p>
        \[
        n = 8 + 2 = 10
        \]
        <p>Therefore, the polygon has <strong>10 sides</strong>.</p>
    </blockquote>
</details>

- What is the name of the polygon?
<details>
    <summary class="solution">Show Answer</summary>
    <blockquote class="explanation">
        Since it has <strong>10 sides</strong>, therefore it is a <strong>decagon</strong>.
    </blockquote>
</details>

### Example 5

The measure of one interior angle of a regular polygon is $ 140 ^\circ $. How many sides does the polygon have?

- How many sides does the polygon have?
<details>
    <summary class="solution">Show Answer</summary>
    <blockquote class="explanation">
        <p>Use the formula for the sum of the interior angles of a polygon:</p>
        \[
        \text{Sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ
        \]
        <p>Since the polygon is regular, the sum of the interior angles can also be expressed as:</p>
        \[
        \text{Sum of interior angles} = n \times 140^\circ
        \]
        <p>Set the two expressions for the sum of the interior angles equal to each other:</p>
        \[
        (n - 2) \times 180^\circ = n \times 140^\circ
        \]
        <p>Expand both sides:</p>
        \[
        180n - 360 = 140n
        \]
        <p>Simplify by subtracting \( 140n \) from both sides:</p>
        \[
        40n - 360 = 0
        \]
        <p>Add \( 360 \) to both sides:</p>
        \[
        40n = 360
        \]
        <p>Divide both sides by \( 40 \):</p>
        \[
        n = \frac{360}{40} = 9
        \]
        <p>Therefore, the polygon has <strong>9 sides</strong>.</p>
    </blockquote>
</details>
- What is the name of the polygon?
<details>
    <summary class="solution">Show Answer</summary>
    <blockquote class="explanation">
        Since it has <strong>9 sides</strong>, therefore it is a <strong>nonagon</strong>.
    </blockquote>
</details>
- What is the measure of one of its exterior angle?
<details>
    <summary class="solution">Show Answer</summary>
    <blockquote class="explanation">
        <p>Recall that the sum of the exterior angles of any polygon is always \( 360^\circ \), and all exterior angles in a regular polygon are equal. To find the measure of one exterior angle, divide \( 360^\circ \) by the number of sides:</p>
        \[
        \text{One exterior angle} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}
        \]
        <p>Step 3: Substitute \( n = 9 \):</p>
        \[
        \text{One exterior angle} = \frac{360^\circ}{9} = 40^\circ
        \]
        <p>Therefore, the measure of one exterior angle is <strong>\( 40^\circ \)</strong>.</p>
    </blockquote>
</details>

## Summary

In this lesson, you learned how to calculate the number of sides, the measures of interior angles as well as exterior angles of a polygon. Understanding these concepts helps us see the mathematical patterns in geometric shapes and prepares us for more advanced topics in geometry. Remember, the sum of the interior angles depends on the number of sides and is given by $ (n - 2) \times 180^\circ $ where $ n $ is the number of sides. Additionally, the sum of the exterior angles of any polygon is always $ 360^\circ $. With practice, you’ll master these formulas and be able to solve problems involving polygons with confidence!
