## Angle Pairs

Now that you’ve recalled what an angle is, let’s move on to learning about angle pairs. These are pairs of angles that have special relationships. These include complementary angles, supplementary angles, adjacent angles, linear pairs, and vertical angles.

<h3 style="visibility: hidden; font-size: 0.1px; margin: 0;" id="complementary-angles">Complementary Angles</h3>
> **Complementary Angles** 
> <br> **Definition**: Two angles are **complementary** if the sum of their measures is exactly 90°. 
> <br> **Example**: If one angle measures 30°, its complementary must measure 60° since \(30° + 60° = 90°\).
{: .prompt-info }

<h3 style="visibility: hidden; font-size: 0.1px; margin: 0;" id="supplementary-angles">Supplementary Angles</h3>
> **Supplementary Angles** 
> <br> **Definition**: Two angles are **supplementary** if the sum of their measures is exactly 180°. 
> <br> **Example**: If one angle measures 110°, its supplementary must measure 70° since \(110° + 70° = 180°\).
{: .prompt-info }

<h3 style="visibility: hidden; font-size: 0.1px; margin: 0;" id="linear-pair">Linear Pair</h3>
> **Linear Pair** 
> <br> **Definition**: A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line. Together, these angles add up to 180°. 
> <br> **Example**: When two lines intersect, they create adjacent angles that are linear pairs. If one angle measures 120°, the other in the pair will measure 60°.
{: .prompt-info }

<h3 style="visibility: hidden; font-size: 0.1px; margin: 0;" id="vertical-angles">Vertical Angles</h3>
> **Vertical Angles**
> <br> **Definition**: Vertical angles are pairs of opposite angles formed when two lines intersect. These angles are always equal in measure.
